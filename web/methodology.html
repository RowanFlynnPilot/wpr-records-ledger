The Records Ledger

Methodology

Every rule the ledger uses, the values behind them, and the holidays it skips.

What the numbers are

  • Our own experience. The ledger logs every public records request Wausau Pilot & Review files and every reply it gets. It describes how agencies have answered one newsroom, not how they treat other requesters.
  • Working days held. Counted from the day a request is filed up to, but not including, the day of the agency's final response: the records, a denial, or a statement that no records exist. Weekends and the agency's holidays, listed below, are skipped. So are the days the agency was waiting on us, from its request for clarification to our answer, and from its fee quote to our payment or dispute. An interim batch of records does not stop the count. A request we withdraw stops it on the day we withdraw.
  • Simple and complex. A simple request seeks a limited number of easily identifiable records, the Department of Justice's wording. The reporter who filed a request never classifies it; a second staff member does.
  • The quarterly audit. Each quarter WPR files one simple request, worded identically, with every records custodian the Closed Door Ledger covers. Those requests are counted like any other and marked with their round, so a round's answers can be compared side by side.
  • Unlisted requests. A request tied to a story we haven't published is not listed, and it enters the agency figures only once it is days old, even if it has closed, so a nightly count can't reveal it.

The 10-day benchmark, and its limits

Wisconsin law sets no deadline. It requires an agency to respond "as soon as practicable and without delay," s. 19.35(4)(a). The Department of Justice's position is that about working days is generally reasonable for a simple request. The ledger uses that figure as a benchmark for simple requests only. It is guidance, not a legal deadline, and missing it is not by itself a violation.

  • Met: the final response came within working days held.
  • Missed: more than working days held, whether the request is still open or we withdrew it after that point; or the agency sent a s. 19.356 notice to an employee after that point.
  • Not judged: complex and unclassified requests; requests where the agency sent a s. 19.356 notice within the first working days, since the statute then bars release for 12 days, and for as long as the employee's suit to block it is pending, s. 19.356(5); and requests withdrawn or still open inside the first working days.
  • The median of working days held covers the simple requests the agency answered that the benchmark judges, so both rest on the same footing.

Denials and fees

  • Denials. A written request must get a written denial that gives specific reasons and says the denial can be reviewed by mandamus or on application to the district attorney or the attorney general, s. 19.35(4)(b). The ledger counts denials of written requests that state no reason or give no such notice. An oral request is not held to those rules. When an agency later releases everything a denial withheld, on review, appeal or court order, the request is shown as "denied, later released"; it still counts as a denial, measured to the day of the denial.
  • Fees. An agency may charge only the actual, necessary and direct cost of a request, s. 19.35(3). A location fee is allowed only when the location cost alone is $50 or more, so the ledger counts requests with an itemized quote carrying a smaller location charge. A fee shown as waived is one the agency quoted and then dropped. When an agency revises a quote, the latest quote is the one shown.

When several things happen on one day

The ledger records the day each thing happened, not the time. When an agency and WPR both act on the same day, the ledger puts the day's events in the order they must have happened:

  • An agency's question comes before WPR's answer to it, and WPR never answers something that wasn't asked.
  • When WPR answers an earlier question on the same day the agency asks another, the new question comes first, unless the answer was a dispute of a fee: a dispute is what prompts a revised quote. Where only one order fits what happened on later days, that order is used.
  • On the day of an agency's final response, a fee quoted in the same letter is treated as a bill for what came with it, so it doesn't pause the count.

These rules set the order a request's timeline lists its events in, and which days count as the agency waiting on WPR, so they can affect the working-day count. They are written out in law.py below and tested against a second, independent version of the count on tens of thousands of generated histories.

Reference numbers

Each listed request has a reference number, such as 2026-10-05-city-of-wausau-1: the day it was filed, the agency, and its place among that agency's listed requests that day. It is assigned the day the request is first listed and never changes, so a number cited in a story always points to the same request. It joins a request to its timeline in the downloads. A gap in the numbering, such as a -2 with no -1, means a request filed that day was listed once and later taken off the list.

Small samples

A median or share appears only when the requests it rests on number at least , counted separately for each figure. That number is always shown. Agencies are listed alphabetically and never ranked.

The data files

Each table downloads as CSV and JSON with the same fields. In CSV, true and false are written out and an empty cell means no value. Amounts are in cents. Only listed requests appear in requests and request_events; the scorecard also counts unlisted requests once they are 90 days old.
