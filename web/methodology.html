The Records Ledger

Methodology

Every rule the ledger uses, the values behind them, and the holidays it skips.

What the numbers are

  • Our own experience. The ledger logs every public records request Wausau Pilot & Review files and every reply it gets. It describes how agencies have answered one newsroom, not how they treat other requesters.
  • Working days held. Counted from the day a request is filed up to, but not including, the day of the agency's final response: the records, a denial, or a statement that no records exist. Weekends and the holidays listed below are skipped. So are the days the agency was waiting on us, from its request for clarification to our answer, and from its fee quote to our payment or dispute. An interim batch of records does not stop the count. A request we withdraw stops it on the day we withdraw.
  • Simple and complex. A simple request seeks a limited number of easily identifiable records, the Department of Justice's wording. The reporter who filed a request never classifies it; a second staff member does.
  • Unlisted requests. A request tied to a story we haven't published is not listed, and it enters the agency figures only once it is closed or days old, so a nightly count can't reveal it.

The 10-day benchmark, and its limits

Wisconsin law sets no deadline. It requires an agency to respond "as soon as practicable and without delay," s. 19.35(4)(a). The Department of Justice's position is that about working days is generally reasonable for a simple request. The ledger uses that figure as a benchmark for simple requests only. It is guidance, not a legal deadline, and missing it is not by itself a violation.

  • Met: the final response came within working days held.
  • Missed: more than working days held, whether the request is still open or we withdrew it after that point; or the agency sent a s. 19.356 notice to an employee after that point.
  • Not judged: complex and unclassified requests; requests where the agency sent a s. 19.356 notice within the first working days, since the statute then bars release for 12 days; and requests withdrawn or still open inside the first working days.
  • The median of working days held covers the simple requests the agency answered that the benchmark judges, so both rest on the same footing.

Denials and fees

  • Denials. A written request must get a written denial that gives specific reasons and says the denial can be reviewed by mandamus or on application to the district attorney or the attorney general, s. 19.35(4)(b). The ledger counts denials of written requests that state no reason or give no such notice. An oral request is not held to those rules.
  • Fees. An agency may charge only the actual, necessary and direct cost of a request, s. 19.35(3). A location fee is allowed only when the location cost alone is $50 or more, so the ledger counts itemized quotes with a smaller location charge. A fee shown as waived is one the agency quoted and then dropped. When an agency revises a quote, the latest quote is the one shown.

Small samples

A median or share appears only when the requests it rests on number at least , counted separately for each figure. That number is always shown. Agencies are listed alphabetically and never ranked.
